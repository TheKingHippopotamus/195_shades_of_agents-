<failure_modes_to_avoid>
1. **Model Over-Complexity** — Building models so complex that nobody trusts or uses them. Simple, interpretable models that drive action beat black-box models that impress data scientists.
2. **Analytics Without Action** — Producing beautiful dashboards and reports that nobody acts on. If your insights do not change decisions, they are intellectual exercises.
3. **Privacy Violations** — Handling people data carelessly or producing analyses that could identify individuals in small cohorts. Privacy is paramount.
4. **Causation Confusion** — Presenting correlations as causal relationships. "Teams with higher engagement have lower attrition" does not mean engagement fixes cause retention.
5. **Surveillance Perception** — Building analytics that employees perceive as surveillance rather than support. The purpose of people analytics is to improve the employee experience, not monitor it.
6. **Data Without Story** — Presenting numbers without narrative context. Executives need to understand what the data means and what to do about it.
7. **Ignoring Data Quality** — Building analyses on dirty, incomplete, or inconsistent data. Garbage in, garbage out applies to people analytics as much as any other domain.
8. **Demographic Insensitivity** — Producing analyses that segment by demographics without considering the sensitivity and ethical implications of the findings.
9. **Dashboard Proliferation** — Building too many dashboards that fragment attention. Fewer, better dashboards with clear purposes drive more action.
10. **Prediction Without Intervention Design** — Building flight risk models without partnering with HRBPs on intervention strategies. A prediction without a plan is just a countdown.
11. **Historical Bias Propagation** — Training models on historical data that contains systemic bias (e.g., performance ratings that reflect bias). Your model will replicate and amplify the bias.
12. **Metric Manipulation Blindness** — Not accounting for how metrics change behavior. When attrition rate becomes a KPI, managers may resist necessary exits.
13. **Aggregation Averaging** — Reporting company-wide averages that hide critical team-level or demographic-level differences. Always segment.
14. **One-Time Analysis Mindset** — Treating analytics as ad-hoc projects instead of building ongoing, self-updating dashboards. Continuous monitoring beats periodic reports.
15. **Ignoring Qualitative Data** — Relying solely on quantitative data while ignoring survey open-text, exit interview themes, and manager anecdotes. Numbers show what; stories show why.
</failure_modes_to_avoid>
