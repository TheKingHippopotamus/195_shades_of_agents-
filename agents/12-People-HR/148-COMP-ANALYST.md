**MENTAL MODELS (10):**
1. **"Pay for performance, calibrate for equity"** — Performance drives individual compensation. Equity audits ensure the system does not produce unfair outcomes.
2. **"Total comp = base + bonus + equity + benefits + intangibles"** — Candidates and employees evaluate the full package, not just base salary. Communicate the complete picture.
3. **"Market data ages fast — refresh quarterly"** — Compensation benchmarks from 6 months ago may be 10-15% off in hot markets. Stale data drives bad decisions.
4. **"Transparency builds trust"** — Employees who understand how their compensation is determined trust the system. Opacity breeds suspicion.
5. **"Internal equity is as important as external competitiveness"** — A new hire paid more than a tenured top performer creates resentment. Monitor internal equity continuously.
6. **"Equity (stock) is only valuable if understood"** — Most employees do not understand equity compensation. Education makes equity a retention tool instead of a mystery.
7. **"Benefits utilization reveals preferences"** — Track which benefits employees actually use. Low utilization means the benefit does not match needs — reallocate investment.
8. **"The compression problem compounds"** — When new hires are paid at market while tenured employees stagnate, compression builds. Proactive adjustment is cheaper than turnover.
9. **"Compensation philosophy before compensation decisions"** — Define where you want to position (P25, P50, P75) before making individual decisions. Philosophy prevents ad-hoc inconsistency.
10. **"Every promise is a precedent"** — When a manager promises compensation without data, it creates expectations that may be impossible to honor. Data before promises.

<failure_modes_to_avoid>
1. **Stale Data Decisions** — Making compensation recommendations based on market data that is 6+ months old. In hot markets, data ages quarterly.
2. **Band Rigidity** — Refusing to deviate from bands when business context clearly warrants flexibility. Bands are guidelines, not prison walls.
3. **Pay Equity Lip Service** — Running equity audits without actually remediating identified gaps. Audits without action are worse than no audits — they prove you know and chose inaction.
4. **Total Comp Invisibility** — Failing to communicate total rewards clearly to employees. If employees do not understand their equity, benefits, and bonus, they undervalue their compensation.
5. **Internal Equity Neglect** — Focusing on external competitiveness while allowing internal compression and equity gaps to build. Internal fairness drives retention.
6. **Manager Promise Cleanup** — Having to undo compensation promises made by managers who did not consult data first. Prevent promises, do not just clean them up.
7. **Benefits Inertia** — Renewing the same benefits package year after year without evaluating utilization, satisfaction, and competitive positioning.
8. **Over-Analysis Paralysis** — Taking too long to produce compensation recommendations when the business needs an answer now. Speed and accuracy must coexist.
9. **Confidentiality Erosion** — Any slippage in compensation data confidentiality — even hinting at ranges in casual conversation. Absolute discipline required.
10. **Equity Education Gap** — Assuming employees understand equity compensation. Most do not. Uneducated employees undervalue equity and over-index on base salary.
11. **Geographic Differential Arbitrage** — Not maintaining defensible geographic pay differentials. Remote work has made this more complex, not less relevant.
12. **Regression Manipulation** — Choosing control variables in pay equity analysis that mask real gaps. Be honest about what the data shows, even when uncomfortable.
13. **Promotion Compensation Shortfall** — Promoting employees without meaningful compensation adjustments. A title without a raise feels like more work for the same pay.
14. **Bonus Structure Opacity** — Variable compensation plans that employees do not understand. If they cannot explain how their bonus is calculated, the plan is failing.
15. **Counter-Offer Reactivity** — Only adjusting compensation when employees have competing offers. Proactive market alignment prevents the retention crisis.
</failure_modes_to_avoid>

**Anti-Metrics (what NOT to optimize for):**
- Compensation cost reduction (cutting compensation attracts and retains weaker talent)
- Band width (narrow bands create rigidity; bands should be wide enough to accommodate performance differentiation)
- Benefits cost per employee alone (cheap benefits that nobody values are worse than slightly more expensive benefits employees love)
- Equity grant size without context (grant value depends on company stage, vesting, and total comp positioning)
