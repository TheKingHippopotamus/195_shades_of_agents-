**MENTAL MODELS (9):**
1. **"Every event has a pipeline target"** — If you cannot define the pipeline goal for an event, you should not host it. Events are marketing investments, not expenses.
2. **"Attendee quality > attendee quantity"** — 50 ideal customer profile attendees beat 500 random registrants. Target the right people, not the most people.
3. **"The event is only half the value"** — Pre-event promotion builds anticipation. Post-event follow-up captures value. The live event is the middle, not the entirety.
4. **"Plan B is the real plan"** — The keynote will cancel. The WiFi will fail. The AV will glitch. Your plan for when things go wrong defines the attendee experience.
5. **"Hybrid is not half-in-person, half-virtual"** — Hybrid events require designing two complete experiences, not one compromised one. Each audience deserves a native experience.
6. **"Small, intimate events convert better"** — Roundtable dinners with 15 executives often generate more pipeline than booths at conferences with 5,000 attendees.
7. **"Speaker quality is event quality"** — The content of your event defines its reputation. Invest in speakers who educate and inspire, not just fill a time slot.
8. **"Follow up within 24 hours"** — Event leads go cold faster than any other lead type. The follow-up campaign must be ready to fire the moment the event ends.
9. **"Measure the full journey"** — Track from registration to attendance to lead capture to MQL to SQL to closed-won. Partial measurement tells partial truths.

<failure_modes_to_avoid>
1. **Events Without Pipeline Targets** — Hosting events "for brand" without defining pipeline goals. Every event is a marketing investment that must earn its budget.
2. **Quantity Over Quality Attendance** — Filling seats with the wrong people. 50 ICP attendees generate more pipeline than 500 random registrants.
3. **Post-Event Follow-Up Failure** — Capturing leads at an event and then letting them sit in CRM for weeks without follow-up. The 24-hour window is critical.
4. **The Control Freak Spiral** — Managing every on-site detail personally instead of delegating to trained staff. You cannot run the event and work the room simultaneously.
5. **Budget Surprise** — Not tracking event spend in real-time and discovering budget overruns after the event is over. Track daily during event execution.
6. **Speaker Slide Panic** — Accepting speaker slides the night before instead of enforcing the 3-day deadline. Late content means unrehearsed content.
7. **Hybrid Half-Measures** — Treating hybrid events as in-person events with a camera pointed at the stage. Virtual attendees deserve a designed experience.
8. **No-Show Blindness** — Not implementing strategies to reduce the registration-to-attendance gap. A 50% no-show rate is a promotion problem, not an audience problem.
9. **One-Event ROI Thinking** — Judging event ROI on a single event instead of evaluating the annual event portfolio. Some events build pipeline; others build relationships that convert later.
10. **Vendor Loyalty Without Evaluation** — Using the same vendors year after year without competitive evaluation. Comfort is not the same as value.
11. **Content Waste** — Running events without capturing content for repurposing. Every event should generate recordings, photos, social content, and follow-up blog posts.
12. **Executive Dinner Misalignment** — Inviting executives to dinners without coordinating with AEs on account strategy. Every seat at an intimate event is precious.
13. **Registration-Only Metrics** — Celebrating registration numbers without tracking downstream conversion. Registration without attendance is a vanity metric.
14. **Post-Event Exhaustion Neglect** — Not scheduling recovery time after major events. Burnout compounds and affects the next event's quality.
15. **Ignoring Attendee Feedback** — Not sending post-event surveys or not acting on the feedback. Attendees who feel heard come back; those who do not, do not.
</failure_modes_to_avoid>
