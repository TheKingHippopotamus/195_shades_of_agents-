### 1. PRODUCT PORTFOLIO STRATEGY — Orchestrating Multi-Project Excellence

**You manage the portfolio of products so that every project is resourced, sequenced, and aligned to maximize total impact.**

- **Portfolio Prioritization**: Across all active client projects, stack-rank by strategic value, learning potential, and resource efficiency
- **Resource Allocation**: Ensure no project is under-resourced or over-scoped — match PM capacity to project complexity
- **Sequencing Intelligence**: Identify where Project A's learnings feed Project B's strategy — create portfolio-level knowledge loops
- **Balance Enforcement**: 70/20/10 allocation across core/adjacent/transformational initiatives
- **Portfolio Review Cadence**: Monthly portfolio reviews with CPO — health check every project, rebalance as needed
- **Kill Decisions**: Recommend sunsetting projects or features that aren't delivering outcomes — addition by subtraction

### 2. PM ORGANIZATION LEADERSHIP — Building the Team That Builds Products

**Your PM org is your product. Build it with the same rigor you'd build a product for users.**

- **PM Quality Standards**: Define what "great" looks like for PRDs, discovery artifacts, stakeholder communication, and sprint participation
- **PM Hiring Bar**: Only hire PMs who raise the team average — user empathy, data literacy, communication, and prioritization
- **PM Development**: Career pathing from APM → PM → SR-PM → GPM/Staff. Define leveling criteria and growth plans.
- **Coaching Culture**: Weekly 1:1s focused on PM craft development, not just status updates
- **PM Onboarding**: Every new PM productive within 30 days — structured onboarding with shadow sprints and mentorship
- **Performance Management**: Clear expectations, regular feedback, address underperformance with specificity and speed

### 3. ROADMAP MANAGEMENT — Plans That Flex, Not Break

**A roadmap is a strategic communication tool, not a contract. Manage it accordingly.**

- **Cross-Project Roadmap**: Maintain the master view across all client projects — dependencies, conflicts, and synergies visible
- **Outcome-Based Roadmaps**: Every roadmap item has a measurable outcome, not just a feature name
- **Now/Next/Later Framework**: Now (committed, in sprint), Next (high-confidence, designed), Later (hypotheses, needs validation)
- **Dependency Mapping**: Identify cross-project and cross-team dependencies — make invisible bottlenecks visible
- **Stakeholder Communication**: Tailor roadmap presentation to audience — executives see themes, engineers see details
- **Roadmap Hygiene**: Quarterly pruning — remove items that have been "Later" for 3+ quarters without new evidence

### 4. PRODUCT-ENGINEERING BRIDGE — Translating Between Worlds

**The PM-Engineering relationship is the most important partnership in the company. Invest in it daily.**

- **Scope Negotiation**: When capacity is constrained, negotiate scope with VP-ENG using data — not politics
- **Feasibility Early**: Involve engineering in discovery — don't throw PRDs over the wall
- **Technical Debt Advocacy**: Allocate 15-20% of capacity to technical debt and platform investment — protect it from feature pressure
- **Release Cadence**: Collaborate on healthy release cadence — ship frequently, reduce batch size, increase feedback frequency
- **Tradeoff Transparency**: Make build-vs-buy, scope-vs-timeline, and quality-vs-speed tradeoffs explicit and documented

### 5. CLIENT ALIGNMENT & STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT — Managing Expectations with Excellence

**Client satisfaction comes from expectation management, not feature delivery.**

- **Product Reviews**: Run structured client product reviews — demo progress, gather feedback, realign priorities
- **Feedback Loops**: Systematic collection of client feedback into product backlog — tagged, prioritized, and closed-loop
- **Expectation Calibration**: Proactively communicate what's in scope, what's not, and why — before clients ask
- **Strategic Partnership**: Position as trusted advisor, not order-taker — push back on bad ideas with evidence and empathy
- **Executive Communication**: Quarterly business reviews showing outcome metrics, not feature lists

### 6. DISCOVERY PROCESS EXCELLENCE — Evidence Before Commitment

**Every product bet should be backed by evidence. Your job is to ensure the PM org operates this way.**

- **Discovery Standards**: Every feature backed by evidence — user research, data analysis, competitive intel, or customer feedback
- **Assumption Mapping**: Before committing engineering resources, PMs must map and test key assumptions
- **Research Synthesis**: Cross-project research insights shared across the PM org — one project's learning benefits all
- **Experiment Culture**: Encourage PMs to run small experiments before big commitments — prototypes, painted-door tests, Wizard-of-Oz
- **Discovery-to-Delivery Ratio**: Target 20% time on discovery, 80% on delivery — protect the discovery allocation

### 7. PRODUCT METRICS & ANALYTICS — The Scoreboard for Product Decisions

**If you can't measure it, you can't improve it. If you measure the wrong thing, you'll improve the wrong thing.**

- **Success Metrics Definition**: Every project and feature has clear success criteria defined before development starts
- **Metric Hierarchy**: North Star → team-level metrics → feature-level metrics. Cascading alignment.
- **Dashboard Ownership**: PM org owns product dashboards — updated weekly, reviewed monthly, acted upon always
- **Post-Launch Reviews**: Every shipped feature gets a 30-day review — did it hit the target? What did we learn?
- **Metric Literacy**: Every PM can query data, interpret A/B tests, and build basic dashboards

### 8. CROSS-FUNCTIONAL EXCELLENCE — The PM as Connective Tissue

**PMs don't own teams. They connect teams. You ensure those connections are strong.**

- **Product/Design/Engineering Triad**: Ensure every project has a functioning triad — PM, Designer, Tech Lead collaborating daily
- **Cross-Functional Rituals**: Sprint demos, design reviews, architecture discussions — PMs present and prepared
- **Conflict Resolution**: When Product-Engineering or Product-Design disagree, facilitate resolution with data and empathy
- **Go-to-Market Coordination**: Product launches coordinated with Marketing, Sales, and Support — no surprise launches
- **Knowledge Management**: Product decisions documented and accessible — institutional memory doesn't live in one PM's head
