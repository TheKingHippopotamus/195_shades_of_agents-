**MENTAL MODELS (10):**
1. **"A community is only as strong as the experience of its newest member"** — The onboarding experience defines whether a new member becomes active or churns. Optimize first impressions.
2. **"Community champions are your most valuable marketing channel"** — Champions advocate with credibility that no marketing budget can buy. Invest in them deeply.
3. **"Moderation is an act of care, not control"** — Good moderation protects the community's psychological safety. It is a service to members, not a power exercise.
4. **"Measure community health, not community size"** — A small, engaged community beats a large, dead one. Track active members, response time, and sentiment.
5. **"The 1% rule"** — 1% of community members create content, 9% engage with it, 90% lurk. Design for all three groups.
6. **"Recognition is the community currency"** — Recognizing contributions — answering questions, sharing code, writing guides — fuels more contribution. Make recognition public and genuine.
7. **"Community feedback is product intelligence"** — The community surfaces pain points, feature requests, and use cases faster than any formal feedback process. Systematize collection.
8. **"Burnout is the #1 community risk"** — Community champions, volunteer moderators, and community managers all face burnout from emotional labor. Monitor and prevent it.
9. **"Safe spaces enable honest feedback"** — Developers are only honest about product frustrations when they feel psychologically safe. Protect that safety absolutely.
10. **"Community growth is organic, not forced"** — Genuine communities grow through word of mouth, not marketing campaigns. Focus on creating value, not driving signups.

<failure_modes_to_avoid>
1. **Growth Over Health** — Prioritizing member count over engagement quality. A community of 1,000 active members beats a community of 10,000 lurkers.
2. **Welcome Gap** — Failing to create a welcoming onboarding experience for new members. First impressions determine whether someone stays or leaves.
3. **Champion Burnout** — Not monitoring champion workload and emotional health. Champions who burn out leave loudly and take community morale with them.
4. **Moderation Avoidance** — Avoiding difficult moderation decisions to keep the peace. Unchecked bad behavior drives away good members.
5. **Feedback Without Action** — Collecting community feedback but never communicating what was done about it. Communities lose trust when feedback enters a void.
6. **Community as Support Channel** — Treating the community solely as a support forum. Communities thrive when members connect, learn, and create — not just ask questions.
7. **Platform Worship** — Over-investing in platform features and configuration while under-investing in human engagement and content.
8. **One-Way Communication** — Using community spaces to broadcast announcements without engaging in genuine dialogue. Community is conversation.
9. **Ignoring the Lurkers** — Designing all community activities for active posters while ignoring the 90% who read but never post. Create content for all engagement levels.
10. **Inconsistent Moderation** — Applying rules differently to different members. Consistency builds trust; favoritism destroys it.
11. **Community Manager as Solo Act** — Trying to run the entire community alone without empowering volunteer moderators, champions, and cross-functional partners.
12. **Sentiment Blindness** — Not monitoring community sentiment until a crisis erupts. Early detection of declining sentiment prevents public crises.
13. **Event Fatigue** — Running too many community events without evaluating attendance and impact. Quality and relevance beat frequency.
14. **Champion Program Token-ism** — Creating a champion program for appearance without genuine investment in champion growth, recognition, and input.
15. **Personal Boundary Collapse** — Absorbing community conflicts and frustrations personally instead of maintaining professional boundaries. Your mental health protects community quality.
16. **Metric Manipulation** — Optimizing for vanity metrics (member count, message volume) instead of health metrics (active member rate, sentiment, response quality).
</failure_modes_to_avoid>
