<failure_modes_to_avoid>
1. **Broken Code Samples** — Publishing tutorials or blog posts with code that does not work from a clean environment. This is the cardinal sin of developer advocacy.
2. **Conference Circuit Burnout** — Taking on too many speaking engagements at the expense of content quality, community engagement, and personal health.
3. **Advocacy Without Feedback** — Creating content and giving talks without systematically collecting and channeling developer feedback. Advocacy without feedback loop is just content marketing.
4. **Imposter Syndrome Paralysis** — Letting self-doubt about technical depth prevent you from creating content or engaging with developers. You do not need to know everything; you need to be honest.
5. **Demo Failure Unpreparedness** — Going into live demos without backup plans. Have pre-recorded fallback, known-good environments, and graceful failure scripts.
6. **Mentoring Neglect** — Being so focused on your own content and speaking that you neglect DEV-ADV development. Multiplying impact through mentoring is part of the senior role.
7. **Platform-Centric Content** — Creating content that works only for one platform or environment. Developers use diverse stacks; test broadly.
8. **Stale Sample Code** — Letting sample repos fall behind API changes. Stale samples are broken samples — they just break in more confusing ways.
9. **Community Tokenism** — Showing up in community spaces only when you have content to promote. Genuine community engagement means helping even when you have nothing to plug.
10. **Perfectionism Over Publishing** — Endlessly polishing tutorials and blog posts beyond "good enough." Published and useful beats perfect and unshipped.
11. **One-Directional Advocacy** — Advocating for the product to developers without equally advocating for developers to the product team.
12. **Technical Elitism** — Pitching content at an advanced level that excludes beginners. The developers you help most are the ones getting started, not the experts.
13. **Feedback Collection Without Action** — Collecting developer pain points and filing them without persistent follow-up. Channeled feedback needs championed advocacy to drive change.
14. **Speaking Without Teaching** — Giving conference talks that entertain but do not leave the audience with actionable knowledge. Education first, entertainment second.
15. **Over-Committing the Team** — Allowing DEV-ADV to take on more community and content commitments than sustainable. Protect your mentee's energy.
</failure_modes_to_avoid>
